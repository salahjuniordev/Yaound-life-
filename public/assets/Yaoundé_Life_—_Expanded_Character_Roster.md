# Yaoundé Life — Expanded Character Roster

**Purpose:** A cast-design companion for the nine full-body character concept images. The characters are original fictional examples drawn from the PRD’s backgrounds, careers, activities, and NPC guidance. Their lives are not fixed classes: players can choose new careers and goals, and the listed hooks are optional story opportunities.

**Visual note:** The PNGs are generated visual references, not rigged or game-ready 3D models. The PRD’s final in-game style is stylized low-poly 3D; modelers should translate the references into modular, mobile-friendly assets rather than use the illustrations as flat in-game characters.

## Character notes

### Amina — student
Amina is a young adult student building a routine around lectures, study, friends, and finding enough income for daily needs. She can introduce campus activities, study groups, the library, career fairs, and part-time work. A useful optional story hook is a friend inviting her to a study group just as a small paid gig appears. Let the player decide how to balance study, money, rest, and social life.

### Brice — creative hustler
Brice is a young adult creative freelancer who uses a phone, a growing portfolio, and neighborhood contacts to find small jobs. He connects to the digital-work, design, photo/video, and content-creator systems. One optional hook is a local business that needs a quick design or photo job; the player can accept, negotiate time and fee, ask for help, or decline. Do not make precarious income a punchline.

### Marie — trader
Marie is an experienced market trader with practical knowledge, customer relationships, and ambitions beyond her current stall. She can introduce suppliers, bargaining, inventory, customer satisfaction, tailoring or fashion shopping, and small-business growth. An optional story hook is a family contact offering a limited starter stock opportunity with clearly explained cost and risk. She should be treated as a capable business owner, not as a decorative market stereotype.

### Michel — moto-taxi driver
Michel is a working adult who rents a bike and weighs time, fare, fuel, repairs, and safety while trying to meet a daily target. His content can teach transport choices and the moto-taxi career loop, including the possibility of taking a break or switching work. An optional hook is a regular passenger who offers a useful referral if Michel has been reliable. Safety consequences should be transparent, recoverable, and never glamorize reckless riding.

### Sandra — graduate and job applicant
Sandra has completed her studies and is navigating applications, interviews, and the choice between steady entry-level work and small freelance jobs. She connects to the Jobs app, CV profile, interview preparation, appropriate outfit choice, schedule, and transparent qualifications feedback. Her optional story hook is a first interview that conflicts with a promised favor to a friend; either choice should lead somewhere understandable.

### Pauline — nurse / health worker
Pauline works shifts and tries to protect time for rest and relationships. Her character can support career progression, shift scheduling, training, and work-life trade-offs. Health scenes should remain non-graphic and non-instructional, consistent with the PRD. An optional hook is a shift change that overlaps a friend’s important event; the player sees the cost of each decision without a moralized “correct” answer.

### Serge — musician and producer
Serge is an independent musician developing material, rehearsing, recording, and building a local audience. He connects to creative practice, events, venues, social promotion, freelance work, and business partnerships. One optional hook is an open-mic or small show with a booking deadline and competing preparation needs. Use original or licensed music; consult local reviewers for music references and avoid reducing Cameroonian music to a single genre.

### Esther — footballer
Esther plays community football and trains toward a possible club opportunity while managing other responsibilities. She can anchor informal matches, fitness sessions, team relationships, and sports events. Her optional story hook is a local tournament that competes with work or study time. Use fictional clubs and kit; her role demonstrates that football and sport are not limited to male characters.

### Jean-Claude — teacher, parent, and established resident
Jean-Claude has a steady teaching routine, family commitments, and a social circle that may include colleagues and a njangi group. He connects to tutoring, education, family visits, mentorship, savings cycles, and balancing obligations. An optional hook is a former student asking for help preparing for an exam during a busy week. His established life should feel like one valid route, not a “completed” or automatically superior life.

## Shared NPC design rules

- Give each named NPC a routine, a goal, meaningful relationships, and only a small memory of important interactions (promises, help, work, or major events), as the PRD specifies.
- Make traits descriptive, with small dialogue or activity effects only. Traits never lock careers, housing, relationships, or activities.
- Keep character stories optional, with clear stakes, readable rewards, and routes to defer or decline.
- Allow a character’s career and life situation to change over time rather than treating the first illustration as their permanent identity.
- Cast additional NPCs across varied ages, regions, jobs, beliefs, genders, and family situations; these nine characters are a starting slice, not a claim to represent all of Yaoundé.
- Have Cameroonian residents and cultural reviewers validate clothing, work details, language, routines, and story outcomes before launch.
