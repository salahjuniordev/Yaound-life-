1. VISION, PITCH AND DESIGN PILLARS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PITCH
Open your browser, create a character and start a new life in a 3D Yaounde.
You wake up in your quartier, check your phone, decide what to eat, figure out
how to make money, meet people, find work, build relationships, start a
business and try to become somebody in the city. Your story is not the same as
anyone else's.

GENRE
Open-world life simulator + social simulation + career/business management.

PLATFORM
Web browser (PWA), designed mobile-first but playable on desktop.

PREMISE
Start with limited resources. Build whatever life you want: student, hustler,
trader, driver, developer, musician, footballer, creator, entrepreneur,
property owner, or something we did not predict.

DESIGN PILLARS
  P1  Feels like Yaounde, not a generic African city. Real districts, local
      food, slang, prices, rhythms (market days, Sunday matches, rainy
      season, delestage).
  P2  Freedom with trade-offs. Every action costs time, energy, money or
      relationships. No single "right" life.
  P3  Hustle with dignity. Struggle is real, tone is warm, funny, communal.
      No poverty tourism, no stereotypes.
  P4  Lives with memory. NPCs have routines, ambitions and remember what you
      did. Stories emerge from systems, not scripts.
  P5  Light enough to play on a Tecno with patchy data. 3D is a style choice,
      not a license to be heavy. Performance budgets are product requirements.
  P6  Expandable by design. New activities, careers and districts are added
      as data and modules without rebuilding the core.

2. RESEARCH FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Connectivity and devices (Cameroon)
  - ~12.6 million internet users (41.9% penetration) and ~29.0 million
    cellular connections at end of 2025 (DataReportal / GSMA). ~87.5% of
    connections are 3G/4G/5G-capable, which does not mean everyone has data.
  - Mobile is ~55.5% of web traffic (StatCounter, Aug 2026); desktop ~44.5%.
  - Device mix mid-2026: Samsung ~20%, Tecno ~19%, Apple ~17-19%, Infinix ~7%,
    Xiaomi ~6%. Expect many low-to-mid Android phones.
  - Coverage, speed and price remain barriers. Typical fixed download speed is
    single-digit to low-double-digit Mbps.
  - Mobile money (MTN MoMo, Orange Money) is the main digital payment rail
    (~40% penetration per a payment provider's docs).
  IMPLICATION FOR 3D: assets must be tiny, streamed in chunks, cached
  aggressively, with a Low quality tier that still looks good.

2.2 Competition and culture
  - Kiro'o Games (Yaounde) is Central Africa's first studio. Its mobile
    satire "Le Responsable Mboa / Elites of Mboa" simulates an African civil
    servant, monetizes via mobile money, and has a telco revenue-share deal.
    Its CEO has argued that real-life simulations suit African audiences.
    We are complementary: ours is open-ended, street-level and 3D.
  - No dominant open-ended Yaounde life sim was found (absence of evidence;
    do a deeper store scan before launch).
  - Njangi/tontine: rotating savings where members contribute on a schedule
    and take turns receiving the pot ("chop njangi"). Digital tontines exist;
    fraud and losses are documented. Highly resonant, unique mechanic.

2.3 City and economy facts for grounding
  - Yaounde has 7 arrondissements. Areas: Mokolo (big market), Mvog-Mbi,
    Nlongkak, Bastos (affluent, embassies), Essos, Mendong, Biyem-Assi,
    Melen, Ngoa-Ekelle (university), Mvan, Odza, Ekounou, Mimboman, Kondengui.
  - Shared taxis dominate. Standard fares ~250-350 FCFA by day, ~300-400 at
    night per person (sources vary; fares rose in 2023). ~35,000 moto-taxis
    operate in Yaounde and carry high accident risk. One guide estimates
    modal share ~33% walking, ~40% taxi, ~12% moto.
  - Power: recurring Eneo outages, load-shedding rotations and transformer
    failures across many quartiers; shops and money-transfer kiosks use
    generators. Mvan once went days without power.
  - Wages (indicative): SMIG reported at ~41,875 FCFA/month (figures in
    circulation range ~36,270 to ~43,969; a revision was promised, VERIFY).
    Formal average ~120,000-200,000 FCFA. Moto-taxi ~50-150k, teacher
    ~150-300k, nurse ~100-250k, security ~50-80k, bank staff ~200-800k,
    telecom staff ~250k-1.5M. Rent ~50,000-150,000 FCFA, food ~30,000-50,000
    FCFA/month. A majority of workers are informal.

2.4 3D engine landscape (2026)
  - Three.js: low-level, MIT, huge ecosystem, ~168 kB gzipped core, WebGPU
    support in recent releases, React Three Fiber for React-style scenes.
    Not a game engine: we build game systems on top.
  - Babylon.js: full game engine (physics, audio, XR, editor), Babylon 9.0
    released March 2026; heavier bundle, strong tooling.
  - PlayCanvas: open-source engine with a Unity-like cloud editor,
    WebGL2-first, reputation for mobile optimization.
  - Unity WebGL: big downloads, slower startup, known mobile-browser pain.
    Not recommended for this audience.
  - Godot web export and other options exist but carry larger runtimes.

6. GAME DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 CORE LOOP
  Wake up -> check needs and phone -> decide where to go and how -> walk/ride
  to a district -> enter buildings / talk to NPCs -> do activities (work,
  study, trade, socialize) -> handle an event -> go home, sleep -> new day.
  The meta loop: Live -> Explore -> Meet people -> Earn money -> Make
  decisions -> Build your future.

6.2 TIME MODEL
  - 1 in-game day ~ 10-12 real minutes (tunable). Day parts: morning,
    afternoon, evening, night.
  - Player controls when to sleep, rest, work or socialize; time can be
    paused in menus and phone.
  - Weekly rhythm (market days, Sunday church/matches), monthly rhythm
    (rent, salary, njangi, school fees), seasonal rhythm (dry/rainy).
  - Optional offline progress: capped passive income and energy recovery
    (max 8 hours) so short, data-limited sessions still feel rewarding.

6.3 CHARACTER CREATION AND BACKGROUNDS
  Appearance: name, gender, body type, skin tone, face, hair (braids, natural,
  fade, wigs), outfits; kept modular so cosmetics can be sold later.
  Starting backgrounds (each changes cash, skills, debts, contacts):
    THE STUDENT ........ arrives to study; low money, academic duties, maybe a
                         part-time job.
    THE HUSTLER ........ little money, big ambition; must find opportunities
                         and pick risks.
    THE GRADUATE ....... qualifications, but must navigate jobs/interviews.
    THE ENTREPRENEUR ... small starting capital, wants to build a business.
    THE ESTABLISHED CITIZEN  job, home, possessions, existing network.
    THE TRADER'S CHILD . inherits stall knowledge and family expectations.
    THE MOTO-TAXI DRIVER  rents a bike, daily target, high risk/high hustle.
    THE VILLAGE ARRIVAL  new to the city, small network, big hopes.
    THE CIVIL SERVANT WAITING FOR "INTEGRATION"  low pay, long bureaucracy
                         quest.
  Backgrounds shape the start; they never lock the future.
  Options: language (FR/EN), Camfranglais flavor (off/light/heavy).

[v1.1] ADDITIONAL BACKGROUNDS AND BALANCE RULE
  THE FREELANCER / CREATIVE  a basic skill (design, editing, photo, music),
                         a phone or laptop, and a client-seeking loop; can grow
                         toward a studio or agency.
  THE ENTRY-LEVEL EMPLOYEE  a job or probation period, steady but limited
                         income, and a choice to advance or change direction.
  Later: established business owner, transport worker, musician, athlete,
  returning diaspora character.
  RULE: no background may have a permanently superior power curve. Each starts
  with different strengths and problems, and all can reach any life outcome.

6.4 NEEDS AND STATS
  Needs: energy, hunger, hygiene, health, mood, social.
  Money: cash FCFA, mobile-money balance, bank balance, debts.
  Skills: cooking, driving, sales, negotiation, management, marketing, tech,
  creativity, music, fitness, academics, charisma.
  Reputation: per quartier and per social circle (reliable, generous,
  ambitious, untrustworthy...).
  Assets: phone, bike/car, shop, home stages, land, inventory.
  Life state: relationships, family obligations, goals, achievements.

[v1.1] NEEDS TUNING RULES
  - Needs decay slowly enough to allow exploring and socializing; they affect
    the QUALITY of activities and mood, not every action or conversation.
  - Several ways to fix each need (hunger: street food, home meal, friend's
    invite; energy: sleep, nap, day off; mood: friends, hobby, music, walk).
  - Cause and effect are always shown ("You are tired: work quality -10%").
    Nothing silently lowers performance.
  - SANDBOX / RELAXED NEEDS MODE: players can reduce or disable high-frequency
    needs management and just explore, socialize and build.
  - Optional later needs: phone credit/data, household cleanliness, fitness,
    work-life balance.
  - Skills grow from activity, training and courses. Skill levels are never
    bought directly. Traits (outgoing, cautious, organized, creative,
    competitive, empathetic, curious) are descriptive, give small dialogue or
    activity bonuses, and NEVER lock jobs, relationships or housing.

6.5 THE WORLD: 3D YAOUNDE AND ITS DISTRICTS
  Scale: game-scale stylization, not a 1:1 map. Districts are separate
  streamed "chunks" connected by roads and transport.

  DISTRICT      WHAT PLAYERS EXPERIENCE
  Bastos        Upscale homes, embassies, restaurants, nightlife, offices,
                expensive property.
  Mokolo        Huge market: trading, bargaining, porters, wholesale, chaos.
  Marche Central Shopping, trading, errands, business opportunities.
  Mvog-Mbi      Dense old quartier: community life, small trade, football.
  Nlongkak      Shops, offices, restaurants, services, everyday city life.
  Melen         Student life, affordable housing, food spots, hangouts.
  Ngoa-Ekelle   University life, study groups, student communities.
  Biyem-Assi    Residential life, small businesses, neighborhood hangouts.
  Mendong       Housing estate, private schools, student rentals.
  Essos         Residential, shops, food businesses, social interaction.
  Mvan          Transport hub, industrial/commercial work, outage-prone.
  Odza          Residential growth, property opportunities, long commutes.
  Ekounou / Mimboman  Peri-urban living, rotation outages, cheap rent.
  Landmarks and roundabouts (Nlongkak, Poste, Bastos junction) are used as
  navigation anchors. Each district has its own prices, safety, ambiance,
  NPC mix, music, power reliability and event pool.

  MAP DESIGN RULES
  - Yaounde is hilly. Roads and footpaths rise and fall; landmarks are visible
    from hilltops and bends. Steep shortcuts are a real route choice (faster
    but tiring), and low-lying areas can flood in authored, limited ways.
  - Every district contains several socioeconomic realities (homes, shops,
    public places, characters). Price and venue-style differences are
    balancing choices, never judgments about the people who live there.
  - The map is a stylized scale model with real place-name anchors, not a
    promise of street-by-street accuracy.

6.6 CAMERA, CONTROLS AND INTERIORS
  - Default camera: third-person, elevated, orbit-limited (readable on a small
    screen, cheaper to render than free-roam). Optional close follow camera on
    desktop.
  - Mobile controls: tap-to-move plus an optional virtual stick; big tap
    targets; interact button context-aware. Desktop: WASD + mouse + keyboard
    shortcuts.
  - Interiors (home, shops, offices, bars) are small instanced scenes loaded on
    entry, keeping outdoor chunks light.
  - Fast travel exists via transport (see 6.15) and costs time and money, so
    travel is a decision, not a loading screen.
  - Accessibility: text size, colorblind-safe palette, reduce motion, haptics
    optional, simple controls mode.

6.7 ECONOMY (FCFA)
  - Currency: FCFA (XAF). Cash and mobile-money wallet are separate (cash is
    stolen/lost, wallet has fees and can fail).
  - Income sources: salaried work, freelancing, small business, buying and
    reselling, transport services, content creation, music/entertainment,
    investments, property rental, partnerships, one-off jobs and missions.
  - Expenses: rent, food, transport, phone credit and data, electricity and
    water, school fees, clothing, entertainment, healthcare, business costs,
    family obligations, njangi contributions, taxes/fines/"small gifts".
  - Prices are game-economy values stored in tables, tunable live. They are
    inspired by real ranges but never presented as current real prices.
  - Dynamics: district price multipliers, supply/demand per product, fuel
    price shocks, inflation per season, wage inflation by career.
  - Informal vs formal: informal income is high variance (market day, rain,
    police raid); formal is stable but slow with bureaucracy.
  - Debt: friends, family, njangi advances, loan sharks (high rates, social
    consequences).

[v1.1] BUDGETING TOOLS AND TRANSACTION CLARITY
  - Every wallet entry shows amount in FCFA (whole numbers, clear
    separators), direction, date/time, category, counterparty, a short
    description and the resulting balance.
  - Weekly and monthly summaries; savings goals; recurring-payment reminders;
    planned vs actual spending; business profit/loss view.
  - WARNINGS BEFORE CRISIS: rent, bills, school fees and njangi dates are
    flagged days in advance so players can plan.
  - Income categories: wages, gig pay, sales, commissions, gifts, rent revenue,
    event prizes, business profit. Expense categories: rent, food, transport,
    phone/data, utilities, study/training, clothing, entertainment, repairs,
    health, stock, staff wages, business costs.
  - Every price is a game-balance value, never a claim about real prices.

[v1.1] JOB DISCOVERY, APPLICATIONS AND INTERVIEWS
  - The phone's Jobs app lets players search by category, skill, schedule and
    location.
  - Applications may need a short CV profile, skills, prior work or a referral
    (a contact who vouches for you).
  - Interviews are dialogue scenes with preparation choices (outfit, research,
    arriving on time, practicing answers).
  - TRANSPARENCY RULE: the game shows why you qualify, what you are missing
    and how to improve. No hidden dice rolls deciding a job.
  - Each career defines: entry requirements, skill requirements, schedule and
    location, actions during work, pay formula and timing, performance /
    reputation, promotion or contract progression, fatigue trade-offs, and
    optional narrative events.

[v1.1] WORK SHIFTS AND PAY
  - Work mixes short interactive tasks with strategic choices. No full shift of
    button mashing. Once a role is understood, players can use a simplified
    "work the shift" action with a readable time skip.
  - Pay arrives as a clear transaction (employer, amount, date, job).
  - Lateness, quality, customer feedback and reliability affect progression
    without constant micromanagement.
  - Players can resign, switch jobs, take leave, or accept/decline overtime
    (income vs personal time).

6.9 BUSINESS MANAGEMENT
  Start tiny (a snack table, a phone-credit stand, a braised-fish stall),
  rent premises, hire staff, manage inventory, set prices, open a second
  location, face competition.
  Competition factors: price, quality, reputation, location, marketing,
  customer satisfaction, opening hours, power reliability (generator!).
  Business Manager (phone app): revenue, costs, staff, stock, alerts.
  Failure is allowed: bankruptcy, debt, restart with lessons learned.

[v1.1] BUSINESS DEPTH ADDITIONS
  STARTER BUSINESS TYPES: prepared-food stall or takeaway counter; small
  neighborhood grocery; clothing and accessories stall; tailoring; barber or
  salon; phone accessories and airtime/data retail (fictionalized); printing
  and cyber-style computer service; freelance digital studio; photo/video
  service; delivery service; car wash or basic vehicle service; small event or
  music promotion venture; braised-fish (poisson braise) stand; MoMo-style
  agent kiosk (fictionalized).
  BUSINESS LOOP: choose idea -> research the area -> stall or rented premises
  -> source stock or tools -> set prices -> open -> serve demand -> pay costs
  -> review results -> reinvest, hire or change strategy.
  KEY ATTRIBUTES: location and foot traffic, rent and upkeep, inventory
  quantity/quality/cost, selling price and margin, customer satisfaction,
  reputation, opening hours, staff skill and reliability, competitor density,
  seasonal and event demand, owner time and fatigue.
  SUPPLY: suppliers differ in cost, quality, delivery time and reliability;
  market sourcing involves price negotiation; stockouts lose sales but are
  not catastrophic; perishables expire in a simple, forgiving way; later
  unlocks: bulk orders, delivery contracts, multi-location management.
  EMPLOYEES: hire NPCs, cover shifts, pay wages, train skills, assign
  responsibilities. Employees have morale, skill and reliability. Poor
  management affects reputation and relationships (never played as an
  abusive joke).
  EXPANSION: second location, branding and shop decor, marketing campaigns via
  the in-game social feed, partnerships with event organizers or other
  businesses, wholesale and delivery, customer loyalty and regular clients,
  customer reviews, savings goals and business loans with transparent
  consequences and no predatory dark patterns.

6.10 NJANGI / TONTINE (SIGNATURE SYSTEM)
  - Join or create a group (friends, coworkers, family). Fixed contribution
    and cadence. Each cycle one member receives the pot.
  - Decisions: pick your turn (early = need cash, late = safer), cover a
    missed contribution, trust members, set rules and penalties.
  - Risks: a defaulting member, a fraudulent organizer, a delayed payout.
    The game teaches how to spot scams without offering how-to for fraud.
  - Variants: emergency fund, school-fees fund, project/business fund,
    digital tontine apps (with risks).
  - Reputation: reliability affects invitations and credit access.
  - v2: real friends join the same njangi via Supabase.

6.11 RELATIONSHIPS AND FAMILY
  People: family, friends, partners, neighbors, colleagues, business
  partners, customers, rivals, mentors.
  Relationships are not simple meters. Model hidden and visible dimensions:
  trust, affection, respect, compatibility, shared experiences, financial
  strain, time spent, promises kept or broken.
  Examples: cancel plans repeatedly and someone drifts away; help a friend
  prepare for an event and they remember; study buddies become business
  partners; a friendship group splits when two members dislike each other.
  Romance: dating, commitment, moving in, marriage, children (all optional,
  all with real costs and decisions).
  Family life: visits, birthdays, weddings, funerals (deuil), baptisms, bride
  price, financial support for relatives, caregiving, parenting, household
  management. Saying yes drains savings, saying no costs mood and reputation.
  Neither a career-first nor family-first life is "wrong".

[v1.1] RELATIONSHIP STAGES AND RULES
  Stages: stranger, acquaintance, friend, close friend, colleague, business
  contact, rival, dating partner, long-term partner.
  Actions: greet, chat, help, study together, invite out, give a gift, work
  together, apologize, set boundaries, attend someone's event, keep or break a
  promise (or repeatedly ignore commitments).
  Rules:
  - One gift never instantly unlocks a deep relationship. Changes are gradual
    and always give feedback.
  - NPC memory is bounded: store meaningful facts only (promise kept/broken,
    helped in a real problem, hired/fired, attended a birthday, graduation,
    wedding or show), not full conversation transcripts.
  - Romance is adults only, optional, non-explicit and can be turned off. A
    refusal is always a valid outcome. No stalking or coercion mechanics.
  - Children and parenting are a later, carefully scoped feature (care,
    schedules, school routines, costs, celebrations).
  - NPC recommendations teach players about new vendors and places.

6.15 TRANSPORT
  - Options: walk, shared taxi, private taxi ("depot"), moto-taxi, own moto,
    own car (later), later extras.
  - Choices: cost vs speed vs safety vs comfort. Moto is fast and risky; taxi
    is cheap but slow in traffic; walking is free but tiring and weather
    dependent.
  - Variation: traffic by district and time; rain slows everything; events
    crowd areas; strikes remove options. Leave early for appointments.
  - Ownership: fuel, repairs, insurance/fines, theft risk.
  - Work in transport; build a transport company (late game).
  - MVP: walk, shared taxi, moto. Driving is abstracted into a short
    "ride" sequence, not full vehicle physics.

[v1.1] TRANSPORT FLOW AND RULES
  SHARED TAXI FLOW: reach a pickup point -> a taxi stops or is offered ->
  choose destination or route -> FARE AND ESTIMATED TIME ARE SHOWN BEFORE YOU
  COMMIT -> accept, wait for a cheaper/faster option, or walk -> short in-
  vehicle moment or transition -> arrival updates time, money and any
  appointment.
  OPTIONS: walk (slopes, crossings, shortcuts), shared taxi (shared with
  NPCs), private/contract taxi (pricier, more direct), moto-taxi (fast on
  selected routes, safety framing, risk), later own moto/car.
  RULES
  - Time-of-day demand at junctions, markets, schools and work corridors.
  - Rain slows certain trips, but must not randomly punish every trip.
  - Route trade-offs: faster but costly, walkable but steep, cheaper but with
    waiting and extra stops.
  - NPCs can be late because of traffic, and the player gets enough info to
    plan ahead.
  - After a route is learned, offer FAST TRAVEL / skip for routine commutes.
  - Traffic is not only chaos: show normal flow, careful driving, waiting and
    pedestrian crossings. Weaving through crowds is never the optimal play.
  - Delivery and pickup missions with route planning come later, with vehicle
    ownership (fuel, maintenance, repairs, parking, insurance-style costs).

6.16 HOUSING AND PROPERTY
  Stage 1: basic room (shared compound, shared water/toilet).
  Stage 2: better rented room or studio.
  Stage 3: independent apartment, furnish and personalize.
  Stage 4: buy land/house; build in stages as money allows.
  Stage 5: property investment: rent to NPCs, maintain, handle tenants.
  Business premises: shop, restaurant, office, studio.
  Interior customization: furniture, colors, electronics, decor, generator or
  solar, water tank. Home quality affects sleep, mood, health, social events.
  Invite friends over to something you spent weeks building toward.

[v1.1] RENTING AND LEASING DETAILS
  - Property listings show rent, deposit, included utilities, room count,
    furnishing, commute estimate and landlord/tenant rules.
  - Players can negotiate some terms and improve the premises over time.
  - Lease dates and recurring costs are always clear.
  - Missing a payment triggers a WARNING AND A RECOVERY ROUTE before any forced
    move. Never a soft lock.
  - Conversations about deposits and housing conditions are written only after
    local review of rental-market practice.
  - Later property ownership: cash or mortgage-like purchase, repairs, rent
    receipts, tenant relationships, vacancies and upgrades, using simple

6.17 ENTERTAINMENT AND HOBBIES
  Football with friends, watching matches, gym, dance, karaoke, music
  production, photography, gaming, cooking, reading, exploring, concerts,

  Each hobby feeds multiple systems (mood, skills, social network,
  reputation, opportunities). Example: regular football improves fitness and
  builds friendships; music production can unlock paid gigs.

6.18 NPC LIFE, PERSONALITY AND DIALOGUE
  - Hundreds of citizens exist, simulated at different levels of detail:
      HIGH   nearby NPCs: full schedules, pathing, conversations.
      MEDIUM  district NPCs: schedules as time-slot states.
      LOW    city-wide NPCs: statistical simulation (jobs, relationships,
             money) resolved at day boundaries.
  - Each NPC has traits (ambitious, shy, outgoing, generous, competitive...),
    routine, responsibilities, goals, relationships and a small memory of
    important interactions with the player.
  - Schedules: student (class, study, friends), shopkeeper (open, serve, buy
    stock), musician (rehearse, create, perform).
  - Social graph effects: you can befriend two rivals; hiring someone may
    create a partner; reputation changes which opportunities appear.
  - Dialogue stack: (1) handcrafted key scenes, (2) templated/procedural lines
    with traits and context for variety, (3) OPTIONAL LLM-powered chat later.
    The base game MUST work with no external AI API and no per-play cost.

6.19 A LIVING CITY: TIME, WEATHER, POWER CUTS
  - Day/night: shops open/close, crowds shift, safety changes.
  - Weather: sun, clouds, rain, floods in low areas; affects travel, moods,
    prices and activities.
  - Delestage: per-quartier reliability. Outages spoil food, kill shop
    income, hurt sleep, force choices (candles, generator, ice, cybercafe,
    power bank). Scheduled and surprise outages are published in the city
    world state and on the Maps/Notes apps.
  - Dynamic demand: umbrellas and plastic stools sell more in rain; phone
    credit spikes at month end; football match nights boost bars and grilled
    fish.
  - Neighborhood activity: busy hours, market peaks, church/mosque times.

6.20 GOALS, QUESTS AND ACHIEVEMENTS
  PERSONAL: save your first 100,000 FCFA; get a first job; make five friends;
    move into your own place; learn a skill; buy a vehicle.
  CAREER: ten freelance projects; get promoted; open a business; earn a
    first million FCFA; hire an employee; open a second location.
  LIFE: become a known musician; own several profitable businesses; build a
    property portfolio; found a creative agency; complete your education;
    start a family; become a respected community member; survive a rainy
    season; win your njangi round without a default.
  Goals are milestones, not the only definition of success. Peaceful lives
  have meaningful progression. Hidden achievements reward unusual combos.
  A "goals board" on the phone suggests directions without forcing them.

6.21 MULTIPLAYER PATH
  - v1.0 is single-player with a shared "city world state" (asynchronous
    flavor). The architecture is multiplayer-ready (stable IDs, server-owned
    economy rules, event logs).
  - v2: see friends in your district (presence), shared njangi, visit homes
    and shops, player-owned businesses listed in the marketplace, trade,
    co-op events, leaderboards.
  - Needs before any shared world: moderation, reporting, anti-cheat, server
    authoritative economy, and a Supabase Pro plan sized for concurrency.

6.22 LANGUAGE AND TONE
  English and French at launch (Cameroon is bilingual), with an optional
  Camfranglais flavor layer. All strings live in i18n files from day one.
  Cameroonian writers review slang, humor and cultural content. Tone: warm,
  witty, communal, never mocking. Voice acting optional later; ambient local
  music and sound design are high priority and tightly compressed.

6.23 FAIRNESS, RECOVERY AND PLAY-STYLE OPTIONS  [v1.1]
  - Players can change career direction without losing all progress. No
    mandatory path.
  - LOW CASH IS A TRADE-OFF, NOT A DEAD END. Recovery tools: cheap activities,
    small gigs, job applications, legitimate help from trusted contacts or
    family, selling owned items, cutting expenses, njangi advance, a day off.
  - No permanent soft locks from rent, food or utilities.
  - A missed event gives an understandable consequence and never erases a
    story arc.
  - Death, homelessness, serious illness and family crises are sensitive: not
    random shock mechanics, non-graphic and recoverable.
  - Play-style options: sandbox / relaxed needs, skip routine commutes, simplified
    "work the shift", pause time in menus, adjustable event and notification
    frequency, text-only dialogue, accessibility presets.
  - Always have 2+ useful things to do within two minutes of entering the world.

6.24 STARTER STORY CHAINS AND QUEST RULES  [v1.1]
  STORY A - FIRST DAYS IN YAOUNDE: find a room, learn transport choices, buy
    food, find a first income source. Teaches movement, needs, phone and money
    without a long unskippable tutorial.
  STORY B - FIRST JOB / FIRST CLIENT: apply for work or take a small digital or
    retail gig; learn skills, schedules, pay and customer feedback. Declining
    is allowed and leads to other routes.
  STORY C - THE NEIGHBORHOOD CONNECTION: meet several NPCs on a market street,
    run optional errands, attend a gathering, see how choices affect
    relationships and later opportunities.
  STORY D - THE SMALL BUSINESS CHOICE: a market contact offers a limited set of
    goods to sell; pick a product, judge demand, set a price, review results.
  STORY E - THE CITY EVENT: see an announcement, choose how to travel, make
    time for it, and meet someone who opens a new career or friendship branch.
  MORE CHAINS (ours): STORY F - THE NJANGI INVITATION (join your first group,
    pick your turn, handle a missed contribution); STORY G - THE LONG WEEK OF
    POWER CUTS (delestage shapes a work, study and shopping week); STORY H -
    THE CONCOURS SEASON (prepare, pay fees, travel, wait for results).
  QUEST TYPES: tutorial/discovery, job or career task, business objective,
  relationship request, shopping errand, event attendance, skill challenge,
  housing upgrade, community task, landmark exploration.
  QUEST QUALITY RULES: goal, location and likely reward are readable; most
  optional tasks can be deferred or abandoned; no hidden timing precision
  without warning; failures explain why and suggest recovery; major choices
  have visible consequences; nothing requires real-money spending.

6.25 FIRST 10 MINUTES (ONBOARDING FLOW)  [v1.1]
  1. Short landing screen: "3D life simulator set in Yaounde" with a quick
     preview, not a wall of text. Try-first flow if possible.
  2. Choose language and basic accessibility settings.
  3. Create a character and pick a background.
  4. Wake up in the starter room; three OPTIONAL goals appear.
  5. Explore one nearby street, buy or receive food, meet an NPC.
  6. Find a small gig or job and earn the first money (target: first meaningful
     reward in 5-10 minutes).
  7. A phone message or event invitation shows the social/event system.
  8. Autosave happens and the player sees how to resume.

6.26 CULTURAL RESPECT, CONTENT RULES AND VALIDATION  [v1.1]
  CONTENT LABELS: every piece of local content is tagged VERIFIED (has a
  source), DESIGN INTERPRETATION (inspired by fact, simplified for play) or
  REQUIRES LOCAL VALIDATION (prices, slang, hours, routines).
  RULES
  - Yaounde is diverse: no single language, ethnicity, religion, class or
    lifestyle represents the city. Cast characters of varied ages, regions,
    jobs, beliefs and family situations.
  - Local expressions and multilingual dialogue only after review by fluent
    local speakers; no random slang sprinkled in to "sound African".
  - Weddings, funerals, religious gatherings and family support are treated as
    meaningful stories, not cheap spectacle.
  - Corruption, bribery and unsafe transport are risks the game can show,
    never the default way to succeed.
  - Hardship is not a joke; players are never punished for not logging in.
  - No real people (living or identifiable) as NPCs without informed
    permission. No identifiable private vendors reproduced without consent.
  - Clothing, hairstyles, music and food are not used as "costume gags".
  VALIDATION PLAN (content, not code)
  - Small paid local advisory panel of residents (different ages, jobs,
    districts, genders, backgrounds) to review dialogue, holidays, markets,
    clothes, food, neighborhood portrayal and story outcomes. One reviewer is
    never treated as a spokesperson for everyone.
  - Short paid interviews: students, vendors, taxi riders and drivers, service
    workers, small-business owners, renters, creatives, parents, sports
    participants.
  - Playtests in Yaounde on real phones; reviewers mark inaccurate details
    directly on screenshots; in-game "report inaccurate or offensive local
    content" button.
  - Event records for real events carry a source, reviewer and recheck date.

6.27 EXPLORATION, LANDMARKS AND PHOTO MOMENTS  [v1.1]

7. ART DIRECTION (3D)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STYLE: stylized low-poly 3D with a warm, saturated palette, soft toon or
lightly shaded materials, strong silhouettes and readable signage. Think
"colorful neighborhood diorama", not photorealism. Reasons: it reads well on
small screens, compresses well, runs on cheap phones and ages gracefully.

KEY ELEMENTS
  - Modular building kit: concrete block houses, corrugated roofs, shopfronts
    with hand-painted signs, compound walls, satellite dishes, water tanks,
    red-earth roads, hills and greenery, power lines and poles.
  - Street life: yellow shared taxis, motos, market stalls, umbrellas,
    plastic stools, braising grills, phone-credit stands, billboards.
  - Characters: stylized, expressive, modular (body + head + hair + outfit).
    Skin-tone and hair variety is a first-class requirement. 2 LOD levels,
    billboard impostors for distant crowds.
  - Lighting: baked ambient/occlusion plus one directional light. Day/night
    and weather by color grading, fog and sky gradients, not by many dynamic
    lights. Real-time shadows only on Mid/High tiers.
  - UI: phone-style overlay with a coherent local visual language (colors,
    patterns, type) so the interface itself feels Cameroonian.
  - Audio: tightly compressed ambient city loops per district, taxi horns,
    market noise, rain, generator hum, local-inspired music (licensed or
    original; no copyrighted tracks).

CHARACTER CREATOR: face/hair/outfit sliders and presets; cosmetic items are
the primary monetization surface later.

8. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 PRINCIPLES
  1. SIMULATION IS SEPARATE FROM RENDERING. A headless TypeScript "sim core"
     owns time, needs, economy, NPCs, events, relationships and njangi. The
     3D layer only visualizes state. This keeps 3D optional/swappable (see
     fallback in 8.2), makes testing easy, and prepares for multiplayer.
  2. LOCAL-FIRST. Play never blocks on the network. Saves go to IndexedDB
     first, then sync to Supabase.
  3. DATA-DRIVEN. Careers, activities, events, items, prices, dialogue and
     NPC archetypes are JSON/YAML content packs validated by schema.
  4. QUALITY TIERS from day one (Low/Mid/High) with auto-detect.
  5. BUDGETS ARE ENFORCED IN CI (bundle size, asset size, draw calls).

8.2 ENGINE DECISION
  RECOMMENDED: Three.js + React Three Fiber (+ drei) + TypeScript + Vite.
    + Smallest core (~168 kB gzipped), biggest ecosystem and talent pool,
      MIT license, active WebGPU path, great with React for the phone UI.
    + Fine-grained control over what we load and render (vital on low-end).
    - It is a rendering library, not a game engine: we assemble physics,
      pathfinding, audio, animation state machines, scene streaming.
  ALTERNATIVES
    PlayCanvas: Unity-like cloud editor, WebGL2-first, mobile-optimized, MIT
      engine. Strong if the team is editor-driven. Downside: tooling tied to
      its cloud editor, smaller community.
    Babylon.js: batteries included (physics, audio, editor, XR), strong
      tooling, Babylon 9.0 (Mar 2026). Heavier bundle; more to ship by default.
    Unity WebGL: not recommended (large downloads, slow startup, weak mobile
      browser performance).
  PHASE 0 BAKE-OFF (1 week): build the same test scene (one market chunk,
    one animated character, 25 NPCs, day/night) in Three.js and one
    alternative; measure load size, FPS, memory, and dev speed on real phones.
  FALLBACK PLAN: if Low-tier budgets cannot be met in full 3D, render the same
    sim as stylized 2.5D (pre-rendered or orthographic low-poly) without
    rewriting game logic.

8.3 3D PERFORMANCE BUDGETS (initial targets; validate in Phase 0)
  TIER   TARGET DEVICE          TRIANGLES  DRAW CALLS  TEXTURES   SHADOWS
  Low    3 GB RAM Android        <= 100k    <= 120      <= 1024px  baked/blob
  Mid    4-6 GB RAM Android      <= 250k    <= 200      <= 2048px  1 cascade
  High   desktop / recent iOS    <= 500k    <= 300      <= 2048px  full + SSAO-lite
  - Render scale 0.7-1.0 by tier; device pixel ratio capped (e.g. 1.5).
  - FPS target: Low 30, Mid 45-60, High 60. Dynamic resolution if FPS drops.
  - Instancing for repeated props; merged static geometry per chunk.
  - Frustum + distance culling; NPC LOD with billboard impostors.
  - Animated NPCs on screen: Low <= 20-25, Mid <= 50, High <= 100.
  - Post-processing off on Low. Particles minimal. Rain is a cheap overlay.
  - Auto-detect: short GPU benchmark on first run, user override in settings,
    "Battery saver / Data saver" mode.

8.4 ASSET PIPELINE AND DATA BUDGETS
  Blender -> glTF -> gltf-transform (meshopt or Draco, KTX2/Basis textures,
  texture atlasing, dedupe, prune) -> CDN.
  Targets: first load <= 6 MB (loading screen shows a 2D/stylized scene
  immediately), character <= 300 KB, district chunk 1-2 MB, interior 300-600
  KB, audio Opus <= 64 kbps loops. Total game ~25 MB after all chunks cached.
  Chunk streaming per district with prefetch of adjacent districts when on
  Wi-Fi or when data-saver is off. Service worker caches with versioned
  manifests; players can "download all districts" on Wi-Fi.

8.5 CLIENT ARCHITECTURE
  Content packs (JSON/YAML, schema-validated)
        |
  SIM CORE (TypeScript, runs in a Web Worker): clock, needs, economy, jobs,
  businesses, NPC LOD simulation (ECS), relationships graph, events engine,
  njangi, seeded deterministic RNG
        |  (event bus / state snapshots)
        +--> RENDER LAYER (React Three Fiber): chunks, characters, camera,
        |    animation, audio, effects
        +--> UI LAYER (React): phone apps, menus, dialogue, business manager
        |
  PERSISTENCE: IndexedDB (local-first) <-> Supabase sync
  Libraries (suggested): zustand (UI state), miniplex or bitECS (entities),
  recast/navmesh library for pathfinding, howler/WebAudio for sound, i18next
  for strings, zod for content validation, vitest for sim tests.
  The sim worker keeps the main thread free for rendering on weak phones.
  Determinism (seeded RNG + versioned rules) enables bug reproduction and
  server-side validation of economy-critical events.

8.6 SUPABASE (REPLACES THE OTHER AGENT'S NODE + POSTGRES + WEBSOCKET LAYER)
  - AUTH: anonymous sign-in first (play instantly), then upgrade by linking
    phone (SMS/WhatsApp OTP via a provider such as Twilio) or email/Google
    so progress survives device changes. Test per-IP anonymous signup limits
    (mobile carriers share IPs).
  - POSTGRES + RLS: user-owned tables locked to auth.uid(); content tables
    are read-only for clients.
  - CLOUD SAVE: one compact, versioned, compressed JSONB snapshot per
    character (target 5-20 KB uncompressed), synced at day change and on
    close. Conflict rule: newest game_day + version wins, with a recovery
    backup slot.
  - EDGE FUNCTIONS: payment webhooks, purchase fulfillment, njangi settlement
    (v2), economy validation, moderation, daily world-state job.
  - pg_cron: generate the city WORLD STATE daily (fuel price, strikes,
    outage schedule by quartier, weather, match days) as one cached row.
  - REALTIME (v2): Broadcast for ephemeral positions in a district; Presence
    only for join/leave. Avoid per-frame Presence updates.
  - STORAGE: avatars/cosmetic packs, optional DLC content packs.
  - REGION: Supabase has no African region; choose Paris (eu-west-3) or
    Frankfurt. Expect ~150-250 ms round trips => local-first design.
  - STATIC ASSETS: serve game bundle and 3D assets from a CDN (Cloudflare
    Pages/R2 or similar), NOT from Supabase, to protect egress.

  PLAN LIMITS THAT SHAPE THE DESIGN (verify on supabase.com/pricing)
                              FREE            PRO ($25/mo base)
  Database                    500 MB          8 GB included
  Egress                      5 GB            higher, usage-billed
  Monthly active users        50,000          higher
  Realtime connections        200             500 (to 10,000 without spend cap)
  Realtime messages/sec       100             500 (to 2,500 without spend cap)
  Presence messages/sec       20              50 (to 1,000 without spend cap)
  Inactivity                  pauses after 1 week   no pause
  Back-of-envelope: at ~20 KB per save, 500 MB holds ~25,000 saves; 8 GB
  holds ~400,000. Compress saves and keep history small.
  Conclusion: Free is fine for prototyping and closed tests; move to PRO
  before public beta (auto-pause and limits).

8.7 PAYMENTS
  MTN MoMo and Orange Money through an aggregator (Notch Pay, Campay or
  Fapshi), webhook -> Edge Function verifies -> writes purchases -> grants
  item with service role. Card (for diaspora) via a provider available to
  you. Never trust the client for grants.

8.8 HOSTING, OBSERVABILITY, QA
  Cloudflare Pages (or similar) + CDN; GitHub Actions CI with size and
  performance budgets that fail builds; Sentry for errors; PostHog (or own
  events table) for funnels and retention; feature flags for live ops.
  Device lab: Tecno (Spark/Camon class), Infinix Hot class, Samsung A-series,
  an older iPhone, plus network throttling (3G/slow 4G) and CPU throttling.

8.9 SECURITY AND ANTI-CHEAT
  - Single-player progress is client-simulated for responsiveness.
  - Anything with real-world value or cross-player impact is server
    authoritative: purchases, premium items, njangi settlement (v2),
    leaderboards, player trade (v2).
  - Ledger table plus deterministic replay or bounds checks validate scores
    before they reach leaderboards.
  - Rate limits, input validation, RLS everywhere, secrets only in Edge
    Functions, dependency pinning (script versions pinned and at least two
    weeks old).

11. SAFETY, CONTENT, LEGAL AND PRIVACY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Fictional institutions, brands and parties. No real politicians, parties
    or living public figures. Sensitive current events (elections, regional
    conflict) are omitted or clearly fictionalized.

  - Scams, tontine fraud, bribery and violence appear as risks and
    consequences, never as how-to guidance. No glorification of crime.
  - Age rating target: 13+. No explicit content. Romance stays tasteful.
  - Privacy: collect minimal data; phone numbers only on account upgrade;
    delete-my-data flow; data-protection compliance in applicable regions.
  - Moderation and reporting required before any chat, UGC or player trade.
  - IP: original assets/music or properly licensed; avoid real brand logos
    and copyrighted songs; use fictional brands for banks/telcos/apps.
  - Real-world prices and wages are inspirations, not claims.
