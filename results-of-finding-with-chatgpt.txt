1. PRODUCT VISION

Yaoundé Life should become a playable digital representation of Yaoundé.

Players should be able to explore a world whose geographical foundation corresponds to the real city.

The geographical source of truth must determine:

- Road locations and directions.
- Road curves and intersections.
- Roundabouts and junctions.
- Pedestrian paths and connecting streets.
- Building footprints and approximate positions.
- Terrain elevations and slopes.
- Water bodies where verified.
- Neighborhood geometry.
- Recognizable landmarks.
- Verified local points of interest.

The game must then transform this real geographical information into a beautiful, coherent, low-poly 3D environment.

We are not building a Google Maps clone.

We are building an actual game world based on real geographical data.

The player should eventually experience moments such as:

"Wait, that is actually the intersection I know in Yaoundé!"

This recognition is one of the central product objectives.

Locked visual direction

Camera: THIRD-PERSON.

Visual style: LOW-POLY / STYLIZED.

The world should have:

- Clean geometric shapes.
- Beautiful proportions.
- Deliberate colour palettes.
- Recognizable architecture.
- Attractive lighting.
- Stylized vegetation.
- Detailed but lightweight streets.
- Distinctive Cameroonian environmental details.
- Consistent visual quality across neighborhoods.

Low-poly must not mean primitive, empty, or visually careless.

The geometry can be lightweight while the composition, materials, placement, lighting, and environmental storytelling make the world feel rich.

Use the existing third-person experience as the foundation. Do not replace it with an isometric or top-down game.

5. COORDINATE SYSTEM AND WORLD SCALE

Use a geographically appropriate projection.

Yaoundé is approximately 3.85° N and 11.5° E.

Evaluate UTM Zone 32N (EPSG:32632) as the local projected coordinate system.

Verify the selected projection and actual dataset bounds.

Recommended game coordinate convention:

X = East
Y = Vertical elevation
Z = South

North should correspond to negative Z to remain compatible with the current game convention.

Use:

gameX = (projectedEast - originEast) * unitsPerMeter

gameZ = -(projectedNorth - originNorth) * unitsPerMeter

Locked initial scale:

1 geographical metre ≈ 1 game-world unit.

Use Float64 during geographical processing and coordinate conversion.

Use Float32 or quantized coordinates for compact runtime geometry where safe.

Store in the world manifest:

- Coordinate reference system.
- Geographical origin.
- World origin in projected coordinates.
- Units per metre.
- Rotation, if applicable.
- World bounds.
- Chunk size.
- Terrain parameters.
- Source versions.

The transform must support converting between geographical coordinates and game coordinates for:

- Map authoring.
- Landmark placement.
- Debugging.
- Player location inspection.
- Future GPS-related features.

Add automated tests for projection round trips, axis orientation, bounds and numerical stability.

Do not compress the real road layout to preserve the old miniature diorama.

The current simulation travel system is a separate abstraction. Preserve it initially.

Long journeys between neighborhoods can continue using the existing travel system rather than forcing the avatar to physically walk every kilometre.
